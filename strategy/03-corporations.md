# Strategy KB 3 — Corporations (base + Corporate Era, 4–5p casual)

Tiers assume a 4–5 player casual table, no draft variant, Tharsis,
base + Corporate Era only. Competitive data cited below is mostly 3p BGA
(no preludes) — where it disagrees with 5p reality, the 5p reading wins.
Starting-resource facts verified against the open-source TM implementation.
Every start includes the corp's MC; TR starts at 20; income = TR + MC prod.

## Credicor — Tier S
- Start: 57 MC. Effect: after paying for a card or standard project with
  BASE cost ≥20 MC, gain 4 MC back (greenery SP 23→net 19, city SP 25→21;
  aquifer 18 does NOT qualify).
- Feeds: MC ENGINE primarily; corp-agnostic — works with any hand.
- Shines with: expensive cards (≥20 MC) in the opening hand, standard-
  project endgames. Top of BGA 3p win rates (42.6%); no source rates it low.
- Mistakes: buying only cheap cards (effect never triggers); hoarding MC
  instead of converting the head start into production by gen 2.
- Pick when: hand has 2+ strong 20+ MC cards, or nothing else fits — the
  best default corp in the game.

## Ecoline — Tier A
- Start: 36 MC + 3 plants + 2 plant production. Effect: greeneries cost
  7 plants instead of 8.
- Feeds: PLANT/GREENERY; TR RUSH (oxygen ladder + Gardener milestone).
- Shines with: plant production cards, Protected Habitats-type defense,
  city cards to self-adjoin greeneries.
- Mistakes: banking 7+ plants with opponent plant-removal live (you are
  THE target in 5p — convert early, never sit on a full greenery);
  tunnel-visioning plants while MC economy starves (36 MC is thin).
- Divergence: competitive lists range S-tier to D-tier. 5p casual read:
  strong (2nd-best historical BGG win data) but attack-dependent — one
  asteroid string can break it. A, not S, at an attacking table.
- Pick when: hand has plant production or greenery accelerants, and the
  table hasn't been hunting plant-hoarders.

## Helion — Tier B+
- Start: 42 MC + 3 heat production. Effect: may spend heat as MC (not
  MC as heat).
- Feeds: HEAT/TEMPERATURE; MC ENGINE (heat is just money with an option).
- Shines with: heat/energy production cards; temperature-requirement cards.
- Mistakes: reflexively converting all heat to MC — pushing temperature
  (8 heat = 1 TR ≈ 14 MC early) usually beats spending 8 heat as 8 MC;
  conversely, don't force a pure heat strategy when the hand says otherwise
  — the ability means heat prod is never wasted, so stay flexible.
- Pick when: hand has heat/energy production, or you want a safe flexible
  economy and the temperature track is uncontested.

## Interplanetary Cinematics — Tier B+
- Start: 30 MC + 20 steel. Effect: gain 2 MC each time you play an event.
- Feeds: STEEL/BUILDING; event-heavy SPACE lines. Builder milestone.
- Shines with: 2-3 building-tag cards in the opening hand (the 20 steel
  ≈ 40 MC, but ONLY spendable on building tags); cheap events.
- Mistakes: buying many cards — steel can't pay the 3 MC keep cost, so
  30 MC evaporates fast; keeping events for the 2 MC trickle (it's a
  rebate, not a strategy).
- Divergence: BGA stats near-top (42.0%, 3p); Steam analysis calls it
  overrated because drafters cut building cards from you. At a no-draft
  table its floor is restored. B+ leaning A with a
  building-heavy hand, C with none.
- Pick when: 2+ playable building-tag cards in hand; otherwise pass.

## Inventrix — Tier C+
- Start: 45 MC; first action draw 3 cards. Effect: your global-parameter
  requirements are ±2 steps (your choice per card).
- Feeds: CARD-DRAW/SCIENCE; supports flexibility-first play.
- Shines with: requirement-gated cards (play "min X" early, "max X" late —
  the ±2 keeps windows open longer, which matters in fast 5p games).
- Mistakes: treating 13 cards seen as an engine — the effect adds no
  economy; overpaying to keep marginal cards because "flexibility."
- Consensus: bottom-third competitively (weak effect), but forgiving for
  beginners — the extra looks help you find your archetype.
- Pick when: the alternative corp fits the hand badly and you want
  maximum information; or hand is full of requirement cards.

## Mining Guild — Tier B+
- Start: 30 MC + 5 steel + 1 steel production. Effect: +1 steel production
  each time you place a tile on a space with a steel/titanium placement
  bonus.
- Feeds: STEEL/BUILDING; Builder milestone is nearly yours by default.
- Shines with: building-tag cards; cheap tile placers (Mohole Area, Mining
  Rights, Natural Preserve) aimed at metal-bonus spaces (Tharsis has a
  metal-rich band left/center — go there even when adjacency is worse).
- Mistakes: chasing metal spaces so hard you abandon endgame VP position
  (cities/greeneries); forgetting 30 MC buys only ~5-6 cards TOTAL of
  keep-costs+plays in gen 1 — steel pays for builds, not for cards.
- Pick when: hand has 3+ building tags or tile-placement cards; you're
  happy grinding production and taking Builder.

## PhoboLog — Tier B-
- Start: 23 MC + 10 titanium. Effect: your titanium is worth +1 MC
  (4 MC each on space cards).
- Feeds: SPACE/TITANIUM; Jovian VP cards late.
- Shines with: 2+ space cards in the opening hand (10 ti ≈ 40 MC of space
  spend); titanium production to refill.
- Mistakes: keeping >5-6 cards (23 MC → 7 keeps = broke); spending all
  titanium without production to replace it — the classic "glass cannon"
  failure both Steam posters and boardoflife flag.
- Divergence: one analyst ranks it top-4 (space cards are the best card
  class); BGA stats put it 8th of 12. Casual read: hand-dependent
  specialist — great with space cards, near-dead without.
- Pick when: 2+ good space-tag cards you can pay mostly in titanium gen 1.

## Saturn Systems — Tier A-
- Start: 42 MC + 1 titanium production. Effect: +1 MC production each time
  ANY Jovian tag enters play, including opponents' and its own (corp card
  is Jovian → you start at +1 MC prod effectively).
- Feeds: SPACE/TITANIUM; MC ENGINE (the effect is passive income).
- Shines with: Jovian cards (Corporate Era adds several), card draw to
  find them; in 5p more opponents trigger it for free.
- Mistakes: assuming opponents will feed you Jovians (they may block);
  buying bad Jovian cards just for the trigger — the card must stand alone.
- Divergence: boardoflife calls it overrated (few Jovians get played);
  BGA 3p stats rank it 3rd. 5p + Corporate Era = more total Jovian
  plays → lean with the stats: A-.
- Pick when: decent economy hand, any Jovian/space presence; it's the
  low-maintenance "solid floor" pick.

## Teractor — Tier B
- Start: 60 MC (most of any corp). Effect: Earth-tag cards cost 3 MC less.
- Feeds: MC ENGINE; MICROBE/ANIMAL VP funding (money buys anything).
- Shines with: Earth tags (Corporate Era roughly doubles the Earth-tag
  count vs pure base — the discount is real with CE, thin without it);
  expensive opening hands you can actually afford to keep AND play.
- Mistakes: treating 60 MC as a strategy — with no production plan the
  lead is gone by gen 4; over-keeping cards because you can.
- Pick when: hand is expensive-but-good, has 2+ Earth tags, or you want
  the simplest possible beginner corp: money now, decide later.

## Tharsis Republic — Tier S (best at 4-5p)
- Start: 40 MC; first action: place a city on Mars. Effect: +1 MC
  production whenever ANY city is placed on Mars; +3 MC when YOU place one.
- Feeds: MC ENGINE; STEEL/BUILDING (city cards are building tags);
  Mayor milestone race.
- Shines with: city cards (Immigrant City, Noctis City), everything —
  in 5p, opponents' cities alone are worth ~+4-6 MC prod over the game.
  All sources agree it scales UP with player count; at 5p it's the
  strongest corp in the box.
- Mistakes: spamming bad cities for income and forgetting the game is VP
  (boardoflife's "hubris" warning — cities need greeneries beside them);
  placing the free city without ocean/greenery adjacency planning.
- Pick when: almost always at 5p; only pass on a hand that screams
  another specialist.

## Thorgate — Tier B-
- Start: 48 MC + 1 energy production. Effect: power cards and the Power
  Plant standard project cost 3 MC less.
- Feeds: HEAT/TEMPERATURE (energy→heat late); engine lines with energy
  consumers.
- Shines with: energy-consuming cards (cities, converters) and power
  cards worth playing anyway; effective start ≈ 55+ MC counting effect use.
- Mistakes: building energy production nobody consumes (energy without a
  consumer is just slow heat ≈ 1 MC each — the classic trap); forcing
  power cards to "use the discount."
- Divergence: one Steam analysis calls it top-tier by raw value; BGA stats
  bottom-third. Casual 5p read: fine but needs the hand to cooperate.
- Pick when: hand has 2+ power/energy-consumer cards; otherwise the
  discount rots.

## UNMI — Tier C
- Start: 40 MC. Action: if your TR rose this generation, pay 3 MC → +1 TR.
- Feeds: TR RUSH only.
- Mistakes: using the action every generation from gen 1 (3 MC/TR is only
  good value mid-late; early it starves your economy — build income first,
  hammer the action in the last 4-5 gens); forgetting the trigger (raise
  TR some other way FIRST each gen).
- Consensus: weakest corp in every no-prelude source (BGA S18: 12.4% win,
  last). Known to jump tiers WITH preludes — ignore that at a no-prelude
  table.
  Slight rescue at 5p: games are short, TR-heavy play punishes engine
  greed. Still bottom.
- Pick when: the other corp fits the hand even worse, hand is TR-cheap
  (asteroids, oceans), and the table over-builds engines.

## The corp + 10-cards joint decision (highest-stakes choice in the game)
Never pick the corp first. Lay out the 10 cards, score them against EACH
of your 2 corps, then choose the pair.
1. BUDGET MATH first: each kept card costs 3 MC. Corp MC − 3×(keeps) =
   gen-1 war chest. You want to PLAY ~2 cards (or 15-25 MC of production
   value) in gen 1. E.g. PhoboLog 23 MC: 4 keeps → 11 MC left (space cards
   payable in ti are fine, anything else isn't). Credicor 57: 6 keeps →
   39 MC still plays two mid-size cards.
2. Count DIRECT synergy per corp: tags matching its discount/effect,
   cards payable with its starting steel/titanium, cards feeding its
   archetype. Rough threshold — a specialist corp (PhoboLog, Mining Guild,
   IC, Thorgate, Ecoline, Saturn) needs 2-3 direct hits to beat a
   generalist (Credicor, Tharsis, Teractor, Helion). Zero hits → the
   generalist wins even if "lower-tier".
3. POWER BEATS SYNERGY on scattered hands (community rule): with no
   synergy either way, take the higher-tier corp, buy the 3-4 best
   economy cards, stay flexible.
4. Typical keep counts: 3-5 cards is normal; 5-7 only with Credicor/
   Teractor-class money AND a plan to play them; 8+ is almost always a
   beginner error (cards rotting in hand = −3 MC each; Planner milestone
   is the rare exception).
5. Keep tests (from 01/02): plays within ~2 gens? feeds economy or a
   chosen archetype? requirement window survives a fast 5p board? Drop
   good-but-offplan cards — "not just bad cards; good cards that don't
   fit your game plan" (BGG consensus).
