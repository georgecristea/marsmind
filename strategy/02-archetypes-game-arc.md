# Strategy KB 2 — Engine Archetypes & Game Arc

## The three-phase arc (community consensus)
1. ECONOMY (gens 1–3): build income. MC/steel/titanium production, card
   engines, discounts. Terraform only when it also builds economy
   (e.g. ocean placement bonuses) or is very cheap TR.
2. TRANSITION (gens 4–6): economy funds terraforming; claim milestones;
   start parameter pushes that fit your engine.
3. POINTS (last 3–4 gens): convert everything to VP/TR. Stop buying
   production. Greeneries next to cities, animal/microbe VP cards,
   direct TR buys, awards.
   - Phase boundaries run off the clock's RANGE TOP, never its point
     estimate: an "end ~gen 7 (range 7-9)" read at 5 players ran to gen 9,
     and pricing cards against the estimate deleted two real generations of
     engine payback — the production cutoff moves with the range top too
     (observed: 5p WGT, real opponents — 06 economy timing). [amended: was
     an unqualified "last 3-4 gens" switch on the point estimate.]
   - From gen 6 price printed VP at ≤ 5 MC/VP against TR at 10-14 MC/TR
     and buy the VP (observed in a 4-player WGT game: card VP 7 vs the
     rivals' 22/17, a 4-6 VP gap — 06 card VP).
   - Last generation: asteroids, awards/milestones, printed VP and
     conversions ONLY — no draw engines, no production (observed: a rival
     spent ≈ 20-25 MC on AI Central + Mohole Area in the last gen → 1 VP
     — 06 tempo).
Common beginner loss: staying in phase 1 too long (over-building) or
skipping it (terraforming poor).

## Archetypes (combinable; pick 1–2 based on corp + first hands)
- MC ENGINE ("buy the win"): heavy MC production → late-game standard
  projects and expensive VP cards. Simple, resilient, strong in 5p.
- STEEL/BUILDING: steel prod + building tags; each steel ≈ 2 MC on
  ~half the deck. Mining Guild style. Watch for Builder milestone.
- SPACE/TITANIUM: titanium + space tags + event cards. PhoboLog style.
  Enables Jovian VP cards.
- PLANT/GREENERY: plant prod → greeneries → oxygen TR + VP + city adjacency.
  Vulnerable to plant-removal attacks: convert the turn you reach 8 and
  never carry 8+ plants (7 at 2+ plant prod) across an opponent's turn
  when asteroid holders sit at the table (observed in a 4-player game:
  −6 VP for one rival, −3-4 VP for another — 06 plants). Ecoline style.
  [amended: was "hold conversions when removal is up" — holding is what
  lost the greeneries.]
- HEAT/TEMPERATURE: energy+heat prod → temperature TR ladder. Thorgate/
  Helion style. Temperature = 19 total TR available — biggest single pool.
  Energy → heat → TR, never sold 1:1 for MC (Power Infrastructure trap:
  observed −3-4 TR for a rival, the winning margin — 06 TR engines). An
  engine with ONE stealable input (energy prod at a Power Supply
  Consortium table) needs its second source bought before the attack, not
  after (observed: the player's backup came 6 gens late, −2-3 TR — 06 TR
  engines).
- MICROBE/ANIMAL VP: resource-accumulating cards. Each needs ~3+ gens of
  runway to pay; count remaining gens before buying.
- CARD-DRAW/SCIENCE: draw engines + science tags for requirements and
  AI Central-class payoffs. Flexibility itself is value — the guide-thread
  wisdom: flexibility > style; have no fixed style, read the cards you get.
- TR RUSH: cheap repeatable TR (microbe cashers, energy→TR converters,
  UNMI-likes) + early conversions. Shortens the game — do this when
  opponents are slow engine-builders (punishes over-building).

## Synergy checklist when evaluating a card
1. Does it feed an engine I already have (tags, resources, discounts)?
2. Does it unlock/enable cards in my hand?
3. Does its requirement window fit the parameter clock?
4. Does it deny opponents (milestone race, removal, last ocean)?
5. VP density: MC per VP ≤ 5 is good buying; ≤ 3 is excellent.

## Milestones & awards (5p)
- Milestones: 5 VP for 8 MC = the best purchase in the game. Race them.
  Terraformer (TR 35), Mayor (3 cities), Gardener (3 greeneries),
  Builder (8 building tags), Planner (16 cards in hand).
  Only 3 total claimable — claim when 1 step away or sooner if contested.
  CLAIM-FIRST: when you qualify and a rival is one step behind and acts
  before you next round, the claim is the first action of the turn —
  before engine cards, production, even TR (observed in a 4-player game:
  a rival played Medical Lab as the 8th building tag with 8 MC in hand
  and spent both actions on cards; the player claimed Builder before the
  rival's next turn — claim-first would have been 70-62 the other way, a
  10-VP swing — 06 milestones).
- Awards: cost 8/14/20 escalating; fund only if you can LOCK the metric by
  the last generation — price every rival's reachable max including
  last-gen production cards (observed: Banker funded gen 2, flipped by a
  gen-10 Zeppelins, 8 MC → 2 VP not 5 — 06 milestones). Never fund one
  you merely lead mid-game. In 5p, second place (2 VP) is often
  accidental value — and from gen 8 count the 2nd-place TIES: one tag was
  the margin in a 4-player game (06 milestones). [amended: was "fund only
  if clearly leading the metric".]

## Multiplayer (4–5p) dynamics
- Parameters fill fast → shorter game → production windows shorter,
  TR relatively stronger, huge engines underperform.
- Attack cards (plant/resource removal): target the leader or the
  plant-hoarder about to convert; expect to BE targeted when hoarding.
- Board: city + own-greenery clusters compound (1 VP per adjacent greenery);
  ocean-adjacent placements pay 2 MC per ocean — small but free.
  TILE PLAN BY GEN 5: the turn you place a city, book its plant source
  (Bushes-class card or 2 plant prod) so 2 own greeneries stand beside it
  by gen 8 — city + 2 greeneries ≈ 5 VP; a city with no plant source is
  worth ~2 (observed in a 4-player game: city gen 3, first greenery gen
  10, −4-6 VP vs a rival's 14 tile VP on the same board — 06 tiles).
- Asteroid riders go on the 7+-plant holder every time; count each denied
  greenery as ~2 VP + 1 TR (observed: 3 asteroids ≈ 3 greeneries denied
  ≈ 6-8 VP, the winning margin — 06 plants).
- Watch what opponents' engines need and deny scarce slots (last oceans,
  milestone slots, contested map regions).
- Land runs out before the parameters do at 4-5p (observed in a 5-player
  game that ran 17 generations: 61 tiles, 0 free hexes, plants piling
  up). When `Free land` drops low, greenery/city cards and plant
  production are dead weight for EVERY player — the edge goes to
  printed-VP cards and resource accumulators (animals, microbes).

## Race margins under uncertainty
Hands are hidden and opponent turns partly unobserved — act on
worst-case bounds, not point estimates:
- Contested milestones: claim ONE STEP EARLY — pay when within 1 step
  and any contender could close the gap before you act again.
- Hidden growth: assume ~1 step of progress per unobserved opponent
  turn on any metric they are visibly racing.
- Removal risk is priced, not guessed: scripts/odds.py removal gives
  the deck fraction of plant-removal cards and P(held) by hand size —
  it decides whether to stop accumulating at ≤ 5 or run to 8; once at 8,
  convert the same turn (06 plants). [amended: "hold the 8-plant
  conversion" is never the answer at an asteroid table.]

## World Government Terraforming (Venus Next option)
One free parameter step per generation, chosen by that generation's first
player after all passes, rewarding nobody (no TR, no track bonuses, no
tile bonuses) — but ocean-trigger cards DO pay (Arctic Algae +2 plants
per WGT ocean, observed in a 4-player WGT game), so they get an extra
free ocean per generation.
Effects on the priors above:
- Game is ~1 generation shorter (clock.py wgt flag); production cutoff and
  "max X" windows arrive earlier; the TR pool loses one step per
  generation, so Terraformer and TR-rush pay slightly less.
- The choice is a weapon: open your own "min X" card, kill their "max X"
  card, take the −24/−20°C heat bonuses off the table by crossing them
  via WGT, place the ocean for your own adjacency (or on a bonus hex to
  deny it), and control the clock (raise the nearly-maxed parameter to
  end early when behind on engine; the emptiest one to prolong when ahead).
