# Strategy KB 5 — Openings: Generations 1–2 by Archetype (4–5p)

Read WITH 02 (archetypes) and 03 (corp+hand decision). Assumes 5p Tharsis,
base + Corporate Era, ~8-10 generation game. Income = TR (starts 20) + MC
production; "income +N" below means MC-equivalent gain vs start, counting
steel×2, ti×3, plants×2, heat×1 per 01-valuation rates.

## Step zero: read the 10 cards, THEN choose the archetype
Community consensus: flexibility > fixed style. The hand chooses the
archetype; you don't force a favorite. Procedure:
- Sort the 10 into: economy (production/discount/draw), terraforming/TR,
  VP-only, requirement-gated, junk.
- Look for 2-3 card CLUSTERS sharing a tag or resource (building tags,
  space tags, plants, energy/heat, microbes/animals, science). A cluster +
  a corp that feeds it = your opening archetype. One lone card ≠ a plan.
- No cluster? Default to MC ENGINE: buy the 3-4 best pure-economy cards,
  commit later as gen 2-3 draws reveal a lane. Corp tier breaks ties.
- VP-only and 3+-gen-runway cards (animals/microbes) are usually WRONG
  buys in the opener at 5p — the runway may not exist. Exceptions: cheap
  VP engines you can start by gen 3.

## Universal gen 1–2 rules (all archetypes)
- Buy 3-5 of the 10 (see 03 budget math). Every keep must expect play
  within ~2 gens.
- Gen 1: play economy cards; take milestone-relevant free actions; do NOT
  terraform unless it's economy too (ocean placement bonuses, cheap TR
  ≤ ~10 MC/TR from asteroid-type events you kept anyway). Evidence: Ice
  Asteroid turn 1 = 2 oceans, 2 TR, 4 bonus resources for 23 MC, the best
  opening at the table (observed in a 4-player game — 06 TR engines);
  Nuclear Power + Equatorial Magnetizer gen 1 = 8 TR for 21 MC over the
  game (same game).
- Any engine with ONE stealable input (energy prod vs Power Supply
  Consortium, titanium vs Sabotage) gets its second source in the opening
  plan, bought before gen 4 — not after the attack (observed: −2-3 TR
  waiting 6 gens for Robotic Workforce — 06 TR engines).
- Book the TILE PLAN with the first city: a plant source (Bushes-class or
  2 plant prod) by gen 5 so 2 own greeneries stand beside it by gen 8 ≈
  5 VP (observed: city gen 3, greenery gen 10, −4-6 VP — 06 tiles).
- End of gen 2 target: income +6 to +10 vs start (e.g. 20 → 27-30), i.e.
  roughly two solid production cards deployed. Below +4 = you over-kept
  cards or terraformed too early; fix by gen 3 or you're out of it.
- Hold ~3 MC per card you expect to buy at gen-2 research; never end the
  buy phase unable to afford your own plan.

## MC ENGINE ("buy the win")
- Buy: MC-production cards (best MC-per-prod ratio first), card draw,
  discounts. Skip metals/plants unless free value.
- Gen 1: play the 2 biggest MC-prod cards you can afford; nothing else.
- End gen 2: +7-10 MC production. That's the whole opening.
- Traps: buying VP cards "because affordable"; income without a spend
  plan — by gen 4-5 pivot to standard projects/big cards or the engine
  was pointless.

## STEEL/BUILDING (Mining Guild / IC style)
- Buy: building-tag production cards playable with starting steel; 1-2
  cheap tile placers (aim at metal-bonus spaces with Mining Guild).
- Gen 1: convert steel into production immediately — steel in the pool
  earns nothing; sequence tile placements to trigger corp effects.
- End gen 2: 3-4 steel production + some MC prod (income +8 equivalent).
  Track Builder milestone count from turn one (8 building tags).
- Traps: keeping many cards on a 30 MC corp (steel can't pay keep costs);
  metal-space placement that concedes all endgame adjacency VP.

## SPACE/TITANIUM (PhoboLog / Saturn style)
- Buy: space cards payable mostly in titanium; titanium production; any
  decent Jovian (Saturn). 3-4 keeps max on PhoboLog money.
- Gen 1: play the big space card the 10 ti was saved for; bank nothing.
- End gen 2: titanium production ≥2 or a refilled ti pool + MC prod
  (income +6-8). No ti production by gen 3 = pivot to MC ENGINE.
- Traps: spending all titanium with no refill; buying an off-plan
  expensive space card just because titanium exists.

## PLANT/GREENERY (Ecoline style)
- Buy: plant production, cheap greenery/oxygen accelerants, a city card
  to self-adjoin; plant-protection if offered.
- Gen 1: plant production down; place early tiles where YOUR greeneries
  will cluster (city + 2-3 greenery spots reserved mentally).
- End gen 2: 3-4 plant production (Ecoline: 4-5) → first greenery gen 2-3;
  Gardener milestone timeline in view.
- Traps: banking 7-8 plants while removal threats exist (convert the
  turn you reach 8, or hold at ≤5; never carry 8+ — or 7 at 2+ plant
  prod — across an opponent's turn with asteroid holders seated: observed
  in a 4-player game, −6 VP for one rival and −3-4 VP for another — 06
  plants); zero MC economy — plants alone can't buy the gen-4+ cards
  you'll need.

## HEAT/TEMPERATURE (Helion / Thorgate style)
- Buy: heat production, energy production ONLY with a consumer or
  conversion plan, temperature-gated cards for later.
- Gen 1: heat/energy production; Helion may spend heat as MC to stretch
  the opener.
- End gen 2: 4-6 combined energy+heat production → first temp bump gen 3,
  then ~1 TR/gen cadence. Temperature is 19 TR total — race starts early
  in 5p.
- Traps: energy with no consumer (≈1 MC/unit dead weight); converting
  heat to MC every gen with Helion instead of taking TR; heat prod bought
  after the temp track is nearly maxed (check steps left); selling energy
  1:1 for MC (Power Infrastructure) instead of heat → TR (observed: −3-4
  TR for a rival — 06 TR engines); 6 energy prod with no TR outlet
  (observed: TR 27, lowest at the table).

## MICROBE/ANIMAL VP
- NOT an opening archetype at 5p — it's a gen 3-5 overlay. In the opener,
  keep at most ONE cheap accumulator that starts paying by gen 3-4
  (count: needs ~3+ gens runway; 5p games end gen 8-10).
- Gen 1-2: build the economy (default MC ENGINE lines) that will fund the
  VP cards later; note which opponents hold animal-removal-type threats.
- Traps: opening with expensive VP engines while income is base-level —
  the classic beginner loss; buying accumulators after gen 6.

## CARD-DRAW/SCIENCE
- Buy: draw/peek-and-take effects, science-tag production cards, one
  science-gated payoff if the tag count can get there.
- Gen 1: draw engines first — they compound only if early; play the best
  production card found.
- End gen 2: +5-7 MC-equivalent production AND 2+ extra cards seen per
  gen. Draw without economy = beautiful hand, no money: keep a 50/50
  split of draw and production buys.
- Traps: buying every card the engine shows you (keep discipline: 3 MC
  each); science tags without a payoff card in sight.

## TR RUSH
- Buy: cheap TR (asteroid-type events, ocean cards with placement value),
  repeatable TR actions, UNMI-style enablers. Skip long-payback
  production entirely.
- Gen 1-2: take TR at ≤ ~10 MC/TR; grab ocean placement bonuses (2 MC
  per adjacent ocean); push the parameter OTHERS also want last.
- End gen 2: TR 24-26 (vs 20 start) while income only +2-4 — that's fine,
  early TR IS income (1 MC/gen each, see 01). Aim Terraformer milestone
  (TR 35) before the pack.
- Traps: rushing in a table of rushers (parameters gone by gen 6 and you
  bought no points); paying 15+ MC/TR early; forgetting the rush only
  beats tables that over-build engines.

## Common opening traps (all archetypes — ranked by frequency at casual tables)
1. Over-buying the opener: 7+ keeps, half never played (−3 MC each and a
   starved gen 1). 3-5 keeps is the norm.
2. Terraforming in gen 1-2 with standard projects: 23-25 MC for 1 TR
   while others build engines — worst MC/value in the game this early.
3. Energy production without consumers.
4. VP/animal cards before income (see runway math above).
5. Keeping "max X%" requirement cards too long — in 5p the window slams
   shut by gen 3-4; play them in gen 1-2 or don't keep them.
6. Ignoring milestone races until "later" — later is gen 4 in 5p and all
   3 slots are gone (see below).
7. A city with no greenery plan: plant source by gen 5 or the city is
   worth ~2 VP instead of ~5 (observed in a 4-player game — 06 tiles).
8. Funding an award in gen 2: it hands rivals a 9-gen target; fund only
   what you can LOCK by the last generation (observed: Banker 8 MC → 2 VP
   — 06 milestones).
9. Keeping cards whose engine is dead (observed: Vesta Shipyard held 8
   gens, Ironworks sold gen 6, Space Elevator never played) or not
   playing a production card the gen it unlocks (observed: Kelp Farming,
   −3-4 VP — 06 economy timing).

## 4-5 player adjustments
- Game ends gens 8-10; every payback calc uses the SHORT clock. The
  production cutoff (01) arrives around gen 5-6, not 8.
- Milestones go FAST — all 3 claimed by gens 4-6. If your opener puts you
  1-2 steps from Mayor/Builder/Gardener, spend for it by gen 3-4, don't
  optimize past it. Claim-first: the turn you qualify with a rival one
  step behind who acts before you, the claim precedes every card
  (observed: Builder, a 10-VP swing — 06 milestones).
- Parameters are shared work: 4 opponents fill oceans/temp for you —
  requirement-gated cards unlock earlier than you expect (buy them a
  little sooner), "max X" cards die sooner (play or drop).
- Expect attacks: plant/resource removal shows up more often with 5
  hands drawing. Don't be the obvious plant-hoarder in gen 2-3 — or ever
  at an asteroid table: 8+ plants across an opponent's turn = a lost
  greenery (06 plants). The leader eats ~3 attacks per game (observed in
  a 4-player game: Power Supply Consortium, Sabotage and Cloud Seeding
  all landed on the leader) — price each at one engine use and carry a
  spare input.
- Slightly favor TR and tempo over pure engine vs 2-3p advice: a +10-prod
  engine finished in gen 5 has only ~4 gens to pay at 5p. Everything the
  2p-oriented internet says about engines, discount by ~25%.
